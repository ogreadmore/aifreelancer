Report 02 / The bigger picture

If budget were unlimited

The right specialists, working in the right order, around you.

Brissa, an exceptional PR team would make it easier for you to respond calmly, take responsibility for your own words, and look after customers. Its first investment would be experienced people doing the work around you: preparing clear messages, handling questions, and helping you follow through.

The priorities would stay the same as in the coaching report. More resources would buy better execution, specialist judgment, and sustained attention. Your voice and conduct would still determine whether the response feels credible.

01

The recommended team, ranked

This is our judgment of importance and likely value for this situation. It is not a published league table of PR services or a guarantee of results. Several roles can be covered by one capable firm. Ranking reflects the value of the function; some lower-ranked work, especially measurement, should start on day one.

1. Senior crisis adviser and personal PR coach

Value: highest • Start immediately

Give one experienced adviser responsibility for the communication plan. That person works directly with you, keeps everyone using the same facts, and helps you decide which questions deserve an answer. Their most valuable work is helping you stop creating fresh conflict while demonstrating responsibility.

What they would deliver: a one-page response plan; an agreed statement; a decision log; and rehearsal of the difficult questions about your earlier comments, Taylor’s employment, and the connection between the two shops. They would practice short answers with you until you can say them naturally without returning to the argument.

What good looks like: you can acknowledge the dismissive wording, explain the business action, and give customers a useful next step. The adviser reviews significant new statements before publication. Routine customer answers are already approved so replies do not stall.

2. Crisis writer and website editor

Value: very high • First deliverable

Make your current position easy to find and understand. The writer turns your response into concise language; the website editor publishes one dated update and a short FAQ that work without a social-media login. Both check that the links actually work on a phone.

What they would deliver: the owner’s update; separate customer answers for Subculturez and Anatomy Divine; accurate profile links; a record of meaningful corrections; and a small edit to website wording that currently emphasizes attitude. They would use the coaching report’s suggested statement as a starting point.

What good looks like: someone arriving with no background can understand what you are addressing, what you have done, and how to ask a question. A usable existing statement is improved rather than replaced merely to generate another announcement.

3. Customer-care lead

Value: very high • Begin alongside publication

Assign a capable person to concerned customers and appointment inquiries, with a clear route to you for decisions. This protects the part of the relationship you can influence directly: whether people receive respectful and useful answers.

What they would deliver: a monitored inbox; a short response guide; a private record of unresolved concerns; and a daily summary of decisions you need to make. Set a realistic service target, such as acknowledging an inquiry by the next business day, and adjust it to actual coverage.

What good looks like: customers know who is responding and when they will hear back. Refunds, scheduling changes, and other remedies follow the actual situation and applicable policies. The team contacts customers who need help; it does not send a crisis email to the entire customer list simply to look active.

4. Disability inclusion and accessible-communication adviser

Value: high • Consult before finalizing the response

Bring in a paid adviser with relevant expertise and lived experience to review the language and the customer communication journey. Ask them whether the wording acknowledges the concern without minimizing it and whether someone can easily find and use the response.

What they would deliver: a focused language review, accessible written and captioned versions where needed, and practical guidance for answering disability-related customer questions respectfully. This is a communications assignment; any wider business work needs its own scope.

What good looks like: the adviser can disagree with the team, and their feedback changes the work where warranted. Do not present their involvement as certification, community forgiveness, or an endorsement of the business. No adviser speaks for every disabled person or for Temperance.

5. Independent facilitator for voluntary private resolution

Value: high if wanted by the people involved • Conditional

A skilled facilitator can help a private conversation stay respectful and specific. First establish whether contact already happened, what was agreed, and whether further contact would be welcome. The public posts reviewed here do not establish the status of private resolution.

What they would deliver: an agreed format and boundaries if both parties wish to engage; a record of commitments shared only as agreed; and a clear end to outreach if contact is declined. They would help you listen and address your response without bargaining for a favorable public statement.

What good looks like: any conversation serves the people involved. A decision not to participate is respected. The facilitator’s success is not measured by deletion of posts, an endorsement, or a public reconciliation photograph.

6. Counsel experienced in crisis communications

Value: high for specific decisions • Focused review

Have qualified counsel review the proposed statement and any proposed public description of employment or private discussions. Their role is to identify concrete wording problems and suggest accurate alternatives while preserving a plain acknowledgment of your own conduct.

What they would deliver: a brief review of the actual draft, a list of material questions requiring your decision, and advice on handling a real legal demand if one arises. This report makes no legal finding about the incident or anyone’s liability.

What good looks like: legal and PR advisers resolve wording together. They do not turn every answer into a lengthy legal argument. Threatening ordinary critics is not a reputation-repair strategy.

7. Social listening and platform-review specialist

Value: high for precision • Start with a baseline

Track the questions that matter, the spread of inaccurate business identification, and whether people can find the current response. Separate customer inquiries from repeated arguments and unrelated businesses being targeted.

What they would deliver: a concise daily briefing with source links, a log of review reports and outcomes, and specific recommendations for correcting consequential errors. Report reviews on a policy basis, not simply because they are negative. Google’s reporting process governs its decisions.

What good looks like: Brissa receives a few actionable findings rather than a feed of every hostile comment. Significant misinformation reaches the adviser; repeated provocation does not require a new public response. Removal is never promised.

8. Media-relations specialist

Value: conditional • Use when there is a real audience need

Prepare for inquiries and identify existing coverage that contains a consequential, demonstrable error or a materially outdated point. Where appropriate, send the relevant journalist a concise correction or update with a direct source.

What they would deliver: a short press Q&A; one contact for journalists; and carefully targeted responses. An update could explain the employment status and point to the newer public request to stop review attacks, without implying Temperance supports the shops.

What good looks like: existing readers receive useful, accurate information. A national press tour, a paid redemption story, or a broad publicity push would not be an automatic purchase. More attention can keep the controversy circulating.

9. Brand editor and local customer-marketing team

Value: useful after the response is functioning • Later phase

Refine the tone and resume a steady stream of real work: tattoo portfolios, artist introductions, appointment information, and answers to common customer questions. Keep the owner’s update available while normal business communication returns.

What they would deliver: modest website edits, an editorial calendar based on actual work, and clear booking information. Any community involvement should meet a real need and continue after attention fades. Participation and publicity are separate choices.

What good looks like: customers can assess the quality of the work and the way they are treated. A new name, logo, or major campaign should solve an identified business problem. Genuine reviews are requested neutrally, without rewards or screening for positive opinions. Google’s policy.

10. Measurement and recovery adviser

Value: useful throughout • Establish the baseline on day one

Help the team distinguish online noise from customer experience and business results. Record the two shops separately so a shared-ownership concern is not confused with a problem at one location.

What they would deliver: a compact weekly dashboard showing inquiries, bookings, cancellations with voluntarily supplied reasons, outstanding concerns, response times, and completed commitments. Compare like-for-like periods where records exist. Missing figures remain unknown; seasonality and other causes remain possible.

What good looks like: the team can explain what is improving, what remains unresolved, and which work can stop. A quieter comment section or a higher review score alone is not proof that confidence has recovered.

02

How the team would work together

You remain the owner and the person responsible for your own words. The crisis adviser coordinates the team. Specialists contribute within their expertise; the writer maintains the current version of the statement and FAQ; the customer-care lead brings back real questions.

A short daily decision meeting: review new material information, customer needs, and outstanding commitments. Decide whether anything requires a public update. Give each action one owner and a due date. Keep sensitive customer and private-conversation records out of public reports.

A simple publication rule: routine replies use approved wording. A material correction, a new claim, or a new promise goes to the crisis adviser and you; involve counsel or another specialist when their expertise is relevant. Save the previous version and make substantive corrections clear.

Professional guidance supports thoughtful audience selection, an appropriate spokesperson, and coordinated communication. The particular roles and ranking above are our application to this case. ABA practitioner interview · PRSA guidance.

These windows are planning targets, not promised recovery dates. Move urgent customer work sooner. A new substantive event may require renewed attention; recycled criticism alone does not automatically justify a fresh statement.

04

When to increase or reduce the response

Increase attention when a new material fact changes the answer, a customer cannot get help, a commitment is missed, or a substantial new audience is encountering inaccurate information. Assign the relevant specialist and publish only what is useful and supportable.

Reduce crisis activity when customers can find the update, outstanding concerns have owners and next steps, replies are consistent, and no material new information needs explanation. Keep the contact route and periodic review in place.

Avoid spending on manufactured support: fake reviews, undisclosed paid defenders, attacks on critics, attempts to buy silence, or a dramatic rebrand without a business reason. The objective is a credible response and better customer relationships. Public approval cannot be purchased or guaranteed.
