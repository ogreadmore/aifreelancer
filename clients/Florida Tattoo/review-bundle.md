## Keep your communication consistent

- Draft replies away from the comment box. Take a short pause, then read them as a worried customer would.

- Use three checks: Is it accurate? Does it answer the person’s concern? Could a screenshot reasonably make it look dismissive?

- Speak about conduct and business decisions. Avoid labels for the people involved, speculation about motives, or debating whether an incident meets a criminal definition.

- Check comments at two planned times each business day. Address material questions and customer needs; avoid responding to every mention.

- Keep respectful criticism visible. Apply any moderation rules consistently to abusive content and spam. Save a copy before changing a substantive earlier statement, and make material corrections clear.

- Share new updates when there is meaningful new information or a commitment to report back. Avoid repeated statements that add no new information.

This approach follows the emphasis on authenticity, timely information, and consistent communication in [PRSA’s crisis guidance](https://www.prsa.org/article/trust-and-transparency-in-times-of-crisis).

**Value: highest • Start immediately**

Give one experienced adviser responsibility for the communication plan. That person works directly with you, keeps everyone using the same facts, and helps you decide which questions deserve an answer. **What they would deliver:** a one-page response plan; an agreed statement; a decision log; and rehearsal of the difficult questions about your earlier comments, the manager’s employment, and the connection between the two shops. They would practice short answers with you until you can say them naturally without returning to the argument.

**What good looks like:** you can acknowledge the dismissive wording, explain the business action, and give customers a useful next step. The adviser reviews significant new statements before publication. Routine customer answers are already approved so replies do not stall.

### 2. Crisis writer and website editor

**Value: very high • First deliverable**

Make your current position easy to find and understand. The writer turns your response into concise language; the website editor publishes one dated update and a short FAQ that work without a social-media login. Both check that the links actually work on a phone.

### 3. Customer-care lead

Assign a capable person to concerned customers and appointment inquiries, with a clear route to you for decisions. This protects the part of the relationship you can influence directly: whether people receive respectful and useful answers.

## How the team would work together

You remain the owner and the person responsible for your own words. The crisis adviser coordinates the team. Specialists contribute within their expertise; the writer maintains the current version of the statement and FAQ; the customer-care lead brings back real questions.

## Compare the realistic options

### Continue with limited communication

This can be the best immediate choice if the businesses remain viable. Complete the essential response in the coaching report, answer genuine customer questions, and keep ordinary business information accurate. There may be no benefit in repeated apologies, a media tour, or a large promotional campaign.

Watch actual inquiries, completed appointments, and unresolved concerns. Avoid treating national attention, supportive comments, or criticism alone as a substitute for local customer evidence. Silence is useful only when customers already have the answers they need.

### Run a bounded PR recovery effort

Buy a defined piece of work with a spending limit and a review date: owner coaching, one accessible response, customer replies, and monitoring of material developments. Have the adviser state what would make them recommend stopping or changing course.

Expand the effort only when there is a specific task that can plausibly help customers make an informed decision. Publicity volume, positive comments, and the agency’s activity report are weak reasons to keep spending if customer outcomes are deteriorating.

### Put real authority under new management

If a viable shop is being held back by distrust of its leadership, consider an experienced manager with actual decision-making authority. Define what they control and how concerns are handled. Represent your continuing ownership and role accurately.

This option has substance only if governance and conduct change. Having someone else appear in videos while you retain the same control is unlikely to answer a concern about your leadership. A genuine sale is a different, more substantial change. Neither guarantees that customers will return.

### Rebrand

**A new name is a weak standalone answer to an owner-centered trust problem.** Customers can connect the new name to the same owner, location, artists, and public history. If the change appears designed to hide that connection, it could create another credibility problem. That is our assessment of the risk, not a prediction of a particular audience reaction.

A rebrand may make sense as part of a genuine change in ownership, management, services, or market position. Budget for the transition and explain continuity honestly. Do not describe the business as independently owned or newly managed unless that is true.

Google’s rules do not provide a general review reset: reviews remain for minor name changes and for new ownership or management under the same name. Larger changes have different profile requirements, and not every business change qualifies for review removal. Check the applicable rules before changing listings. [Review continuity](evidence.html#G06) · [Rebranding rules](evidence.html#G07).

### Pause or reduce the business

A pause should solve a named problem and have a plan for what must be true before reopening. Simply disappearing until attention fades leaves customers with unanswered questions and gives no assurance that the situation will improve.

Compare reduced hours, fewer services, or retaining one location with the cost of a full temporary closure. Have the relevant advisers check what obligations continue and how existing appointments and deposits will be handled. Avoid making a pause so expensive that it removes the ability to reopen or exit cleanly.

### Sell or transfer the business

An actual ownership transition may preserve value that would be lost in closure. A buyer may value the location, equipment, customer relationships, or artist team. Those are possibilities to evaluate, not a promise that the business is saleable.

Get an independent valuation and advice on the transaction. Assess whether a buyer has the ability and resources to operate the shop. Be accurate about what is being transferred and any continuing role you would have. A transfer to a friend or relative that leaves the same undisclosed control would not create the separation customers might expect. [SBA sale and transfer guidance](evidence.html#G08).

Plan the licensing transition before promising uninterrupted service. Florida’s Department of Health says a tattoo establishment license cannot transfer between people; when ownership changes, a new license must be issued before tattooing. Have the buyer and advisers confirm the requirements for the actual transaction with the county health department. This is one transition requirement, not a complete sale checklist. [Florida licensing guidance](evidence.html#G09).

### Close in an orderly way

**Closure is a legitimate option. It should not be dismissed simply because a PR plan exists.** It becomes more compelling when there is no credible path to meeting commitments, the cost of continuing exceeds the value reasonably being preserved, or you decide that continuing is no longer worthwhile after considering feasible alternatives.

Assess each shop separately. Get an accountant and qualified attorney to review cash, debts, leases, deposits, employment commitments, records, and the steps that apply to the actual business entities. Keep a usable customer contact route through the transition. This is a decision to plan, not an instruction to shut a shop immediately. [SBA closure guidance](evidence.html#G08).

Closure does not itself remove past reviews or restore a personal reputation. Google says reviews remain on temporarily and permanently closed profiles. [Google policy](evidence.html#G06).

## What would make continued PR a sensible investment?

Before buying a substantial campaign, ask an independent business adviser or accountant to help complete this review for each shop. The person assessing viability should be able to recommend an exit without losing a large PR retainer.

| Question | Evidence to use | What the answer changes |
|---|---|---|
| Is there enough real demand? | Completed appointments, booking conversion, repeat customers, and voluntarily supplied cancellation reasons. | Whether there is a customer base worth trying to retain. |
| Can the shop meet commitments during recovery? | A dated cash forecast, available cash, upcoming bills, deposits, and realistic service costs. | How much time and spending are actually available. |
| Can the shop deliver the work customers want? | Current artist availability, significant departure risks, forward bookings, and realistic replacement capacity. | Whether forecast revenue can actually be earned. One departure is not automatically a closure trigger. |
| Can the response remain credible? | Consistency between public statements, decisions, and subsequent replies. | Whether more communication is likely to help. |
| Is there a specific barrier PR can address? | Customers repeatedly lack a factual answer or cannot find the current response. | Whether to fund a targeted communication task. |
| Would another structure preserve more value? | Costed management, sale, downsizing, and closure scenarios. | Whether continuing the current model is the strongest option. |

Compare normal recent periods with current results, allowing for seasonality and other causes. Measure the income the business actually retains after service costs; a busy appointment book can still fail to support the business. Ask the accountant to model a weak-demand scenario as well as an improving one. No success probability or spending amount can be responsibly filled in from social posts alone.

**PR is a poor lead investment when the owner will not follow through, the business cannot meet its commitments, or there is no defined communication problem left for a campaign to solve.** Those conditions call for a different decision or specialist. More polished messaging will not resolve them.
