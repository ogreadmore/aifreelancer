01 · The move

Change the game, not the script.

David’s phone skill is real. The weak link is the model around it: rented attention, one narrow offer, and no compounding asset. The new company keeps the skill and replaces the cage.

Today · cash-buyer-first wholesaler

A discounted contract is the main route.

  • Lead vendors own the funnel and the first-party data.
  • Nonexclusive purchased leads can trigger a swarm of competing calls, based on David’s experience.
  • The public site mentions alternatives, but the operating process and economics are not yet documented.
  • “Nationwide” creates shallow proof and state-by-state risk.
Next · seller-exit company

Show the legitimate routes side by side.

  • Cash purchase or assignment when it genuinely fits.
  • Competitive bids from verified investors when properly structured.
  • Listing or referral through a licensed, supervising brokerage program.
  • Nurture—or an honest “do not sell yet”—when that is the better answer.

06 · Experiments

Three cheap ways to discover what deserves scale.

The plan self-corrects through experiments. Every test has a service prerequisite, a mature read, and an automatic decision rule.

Experiment A

Manual Seller Option Report

Test: Can David clearly diagnose cash vs. licensed listing/referral vs. nurture for his own consented non-fit sellers?

Experiment B

Direct first-node demand

Test: Can narrow local referral, search, or event campaigns produce better collected contribution per dollar and founder hour than PPL?

Experiment C

Rejected-lead recovery

Test later: After the service works, can suppliers deliver named, consented non-fit sellers with healthy mature economics?

The rejected-lead exchange switch rule
Recovery becomes the primary acquisition system only when the complete gate fires: three independent suppliers, at least 100 fully consented handoffs, two mature cohorts, two supplier renewals, and economics that beat both PPL and direct acquisition per dollar and founder hour without worse complaints or consent defects. Conversion and dollar thresholds must come from David’s baseline; inventing them now would be false precision.
The Tampa day-45 checkpoint
If Tampa Bay clears the readiness screen, compare contact quality, qualified conversations, option-report completion, channel cost, broker readiness, buyer depth, founder time, and early collected contribution at day 45. Keep the node, narrow to a better seller situation, or move the economic test; do not defend the market for emotional reasons.
Stress test

The strongest arguments against this plan

“Multiple exits are too complex.”

Correct if David builds software first. Start with a manual report and broker-approved handoffs; automate only repeated work.

“Local focus abandons national cash.”

Keep viable national PPL deals as a capped bridge. Concentrate new owned-demand investment where proof can compound.

“Owned leads may cost more.”

They may. Compare mature collected contribution per dollar and founder hour; kill any channel that cannot beat the control.

“Advising and buying can conflict.”

Use written role and compensation disclosures, consistent option math, broker supervision, and a clean seller choice.

08 · Guardrails

Aggressive and lawful are the same strategy.

Trust and compliance are product features. Specific structures require a Florida lawyer and a suitable supervising broker; this report is strategy, not legal advice.

Required before scale

  • An active license under a suitable Florida broker—or a broker-operated partner program—before any brokerage, listing, or compensated referral activity.
  • All-party recording consent where Florida law applies; documented permission for nurture and every named lead handoff.
  • State-by-state clearance, disclosures, and title process before activating a market.
  • Truthful offer math, genuine reviews, clear capacity disclosure, and a clean seller exit.
  • Broker/counsel approval for compensation, advertising, agency, novation, or competitive-bid structures.

Off the table

  • Cold automated SMS, ringless voicemail, or AI-generated calls without documented permission and campaign-specific legal clearance.
  • Unlicensed referral fees, net listings, or pretending brokerage activity is “marketing.”
  • Illegal or misleading subject-to schemes, equity stripping, hidden terms, or fear-based pressure.
  • Counting pipeline as cash, hiring from forecasts, or scaling weak cohorts because leads look cheap.
  • Nationwide claims without real local execution, proof, and state clearance.
09 · Evidence

What is known—and what is still a hypothesis.

David’s reported history is the starting point. Competitor claims remain company claims unless backed by filings or independent measurement. Private-company profit is generally unknowable.

Key assumptions that must be validated with David’s data
We still need the 90-day lead/vendor export; funded fees and true fallout; every open contract’s status; spend and runway; the buyer list and funded history; consent artifacts; and the actual disposition workflow. Until then, market order, channel economics, and revenue scenarios are hypotheses.
How to read the labels
Fact means a current primary or filing-grade source. Reported means David or a company said it. Estimate is a planning input. Hypothesis is something the experiment must prove. Decision is the strategy chosen despite remaining uncertainty.
What David’s current website confirms
The live site is a template-based nationwide cash-buyer page built on REsimpli. It includes a seller consent field and mentions alternative help—both positives—but the homepage does not show a named founder, funded transaction proof, a clear multi-exit process, or locally specific authority. It repeatedly says the company buys houses and pays cash, so David should verify that every capacity, offer, fee, and closing claim precisely matches how each transaction is actually structured.
