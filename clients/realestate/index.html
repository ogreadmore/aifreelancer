01 · The change

Keep David’s sales skill. Replace the weak business model.

David is good on the phone. The problem is paying repeatedly for leads, offering most sellers only one kind of deal, and owning very little that becomes more valuable over time.

Current approach · one main option

Make the seller a below-market cash offer.

  • David usually earns by putting the home under contract, then transferring that deal to an investor for a fee.
  • The lead company controls where the leads come from and can raise the price.
  • The same bought lead may be sold to several competitors, creating a rush of calls.
  • The website mentions other choices, but the process and profit from them are not yet proven.
  • Claiming to work nationwide makes local trust and legal compliance harder.
Recommended approach · several options

Show sellers their main options and help them choose.

  • A fast cash sale when speed and certainty matter most.
  • Several bids from verified investors when the process is legally approved.
  • A traditional listing or agent referral through a licensed broker.
  • Follow up later—or honestly recommend not selling yet—when that is better.

06 · Experiments

Three low-cost tests that tell us what to grow.

Set the pass-or-fail rules before each test begins. These numbers are not promises. Change them only when David’s real history provides better evidence—not because a result is disappointing.

Experiment A

Give sellers a simple Options Report

Test: use 100 past leads whom David is legally allowed to contact and could not help before.
Pass: speak with at least 20%, find a real next step for at least 10%, and eventually earn at least $15 per lead after counting labor.

Experiment B

Generate leads directly in the first market

Test: begin with free problem-specific pages, referrals, partners, and helpful content. Use ads only from completed-deal profit, with no more than $6,000 across two rounds.
Pass: each valid seller lead costs no more than $150, David reaches at least 50%, at least 15% have a real next step, and the predicted cost to get a completed deal stays below 35% of its expected profit.

Experiment C

Later: accept leads other companies cannot help

Test: contact 20 qualified companies after counsel approves the arrangement. Pay no more than $50 for each valid introduction where the seller clearly agreed to hear from David.
Pass: at least three companies participate, 100 valid leads arrive, David reaches at least 25%, at least 10% have a real next step, and each lead eventually earns at least $15 after labor.

When should leads from other companies become the main source?
Make this the main lead source only when all six requirements are met:
  1. At least three partner companies are actively sending leads.
  2. They send 100 valid leads on the same day the seller agrees to hear from David.
  3. David reaches at least 25% and finds a real next step for at least 10%.
  4. Each lead eventually earns at least $15 after labor.
  5. At least two partners continue or send more leads.
  6. Two groups of leads generated directly by David fail their own cost and profit tests.
Any permission problem or increase in complaints fails the test.
How we decide whether Tampa is working after 45 days
If Tampa Bay is ready, compare it after 45 days with David’s two strongest past markets. Look at how many sellers answer, how many are serious, how many reports are completed, lead cost, broker readiness, reliable buyers, David’s time, and early cash profit. Keep Tampa, focus on a better seller problem, or test another market based on evidence—not personal attachment.
Stress test

The strongest arguments against this plan

“Several selling options sound complicated.”

They will be if David builds software first. Begin with a simple manual report and broker-approved introductions. Automate only tasks that repeat.

“Focusing locally could waste national opportunities.”

Finish promising existing contracts, use legally approved partnerships with local operators, and send sellers through licensed professionals—without advertising nationwide.

“Generating our own leads may cost more.”

It might. Compare the eventual cash profit and David’s time with his old bought-lead results. Stop any source that cannot win.

“How can David advise a seller and also buy?”

Clearly disclose David’s role and payment, use consistent estimates, involve the supervising broker, and let the seller choose freely.

08 · Safety rules

Move quickly without creating legal or trust problems.

Trust and legal compliance make the business stronger. A Florida lawyer and a suitable supervising broker must approve the exact transaction structure. This report is a business plan, not legal advice.

Required before growing

  • An active license under a suitable Florida broker—or a broker-operated partner program—before any brokerage, listing, or compensated referral activity.
  • All-party recording consent where Florida law applies; documented permission for nurture and every handoff to the company receiving it.
  • Lawyer-approved calling lists, federal and state do-not-call controls, a check against the National Do Not Call Registry at least every 31 days, and an internal list that immediately blocks people who opt out.
  • Clear opt-in before non-consumer texts; every message program identifies the sender and preserves proof. Consent given only to an unnamed other company is not enough.
  • State-by-state wholesaling, licensing, disclosure, marketing, and title clearance before activating a market. “National” is not one compliance regime.
  • Honest price estimates, genuine reviews, a clear explanation of David’s role, and an easy way for the seller to say no.
  • A broker and lawyer must approve how David is paid, how ads describe his role, who represents the seller, any replacement contract, and any several-bid process. Use Meta’s required Housing Special Ad Category if paid social ads begin later.

Off the table

  • Cold automated text messages, voicemail delivered without ringing, or AI-generated calls without documented permission and legal approval for that exact campaign.
  • Assuming Florida’s telephone-solicitation law—or any other state rule—disappears because outreach originates elsewhere.
  • Paying unlicensed people for real-estate referrals, arrangements where an agent keeps everything above an agreed seller price, or calling brokerage work “marketing” to avoid the rules.
  • Illegal or misleading deals that take over a seller’s mortgage payments, strip away the seller’s value, hide important terms, or use fear and pressure.
  • Counting possible deals as cash, hiring from estimates, or buying more weak leads simply because they look inexpensive.
  • Nationwide claims without real local execution, proof, and state clearance.

How to read the labels
Fact comes from a current official source or public filing. Reported means David or a company said it, but it may not be independently verified. Estimate is a planning number. Testable idea still needs proof. Decision is the chosen direction despite uncertainty.
What David’s current website confirms
The live site is a template-based nationwide cash-buyer page built on REsimpli. It includes a place where the seller gives permission to be contacted and mentions other kinds of help—both positives—but the homepage does not show a named founder, proof of completed transactions, a clear process for offering several selling choices, or strong local expertise. It repeatedly says the company buys houses and pays cash, so David should clearly state whether he is acting as the buyer, a wholesaler assigning a contract, or a licensed agent—and ensure every offer, fee, and closing claim matches what actually happens.
